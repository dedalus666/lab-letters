title: Tommy and the Guardians of Oblivion
kind: poem
tags: poems

date: 2005-09-19
---

<div class="poem-block">
“There’s a bum digging through the garbage in the garage, “ my boss said. 
“Go kick him out of there.” 
I was a deli clerk in New York City. 
I sliced meat and smoked fish for wealthy Jewish customers. <br>
Most of the homeless in that neighborhood were my friends. 
I knew many by name. 
If I didn’t know their names, I gave them ones that I made up. 
Moose, Frank, Dick, Chip, Rasta; and so on. 
Some I named after characters of Faulkner’s Snopes trilogy; 
Flem, Manfred and Mink were a few. 
(They were) the guardians of oblivion. <br>
In the garage
Headfirst in the dumpster
Was Tommy
He sold books on the sidewalk
People gave him old paperbacks and porn magazines. 
It didn’t seem to be a very lucrative business. 
The garbage room was full. It reeked as garbage does. 
Ripe and rotten. <br>
I tapped on the bottom of Tommy’s boot with a sawed-off broomstick they kept handy in the front of the store. 
I could see his foot through it. 
A quarter-size hole exposed the tar-stained pink of his heel. 
He ignored me – but began to hurry it up. <br>
I tapped again. 
“Come on, get out of there now,” I said. “The boss is watching.” 
The smell was getting to me. 
“Anyway, “ I said, “you don’t want that.” <br>
He rose up, greasy and smiling. 
“Oh yes I do. This is good eatin.’
I couldn’t disagree with him. 
He had picked clean the bones of a smoked whitefish. 
Oily chunks of pale-grey fish meat filled a paper coffee cup. 
My boss wouldn’t have been happy that particular fish ended up in the garbage. <br>
Tommy wore a silver chain with a Star of David pendant. 
He was not a wealthy Jew. 
But he still ate like one. 
At least, sometimes.

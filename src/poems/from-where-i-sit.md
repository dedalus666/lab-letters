title: From Where I Sit

<div class="poem-block">
From where I sit I can see planes taking off
Noses to the sky, bellies slanting upward, wings like a frown
Every time one goes, I silently wish I was on it. <br>
Out there.
So many places, opportunities, adventures
Out there, far away. 
Beyond this desk. <br>
At nearly 40, time becomes an enemy. 
It gets away from us. 
Some stop chasing it and begin waiting.
For the end of it. <br>
But I can’t slow down now.  
Things are just coming into focus. 
And I would miss
 the silent thrill
Of the unknown.
The unexplored.
The tiny wheels and cogs
Of this magnificent machine<br>
The iridescence of a bluebird’s feathers
The taste of mandarin oranges
The smell of diesel
The roaring of subway cars and water over rocks
The unexpected touch of a lover’s hand in the dark.<br>
Yes there are lies and  heartbreak
Suffering and loss 
Countless years wasted with guilt, anger, resentment and sorrow
sleeping
working 
Toiling about in the killing of time to get to the good parts
if only for a moment. <br>
I have no regrets. 
Each mistake is a lesson. 
I’m due some more learning 
Dreams and aspirations like glass
often shatter
But they still shimmer and sparkle
Even with our blood on them
And there are
still possibilities in the fallen.
Each shard and broken piece 
A new beginning
The sharpest ones cut the deepest
But it reminds us the stuff of our insides
Is oil on the cogs and the wheels
And they just keep spinning and spinning and spinning.  <br>
The hardest part is sitting still. <br>
Dear Gramps,<br>
I can’t believe it’s been a year since you died. 
It seems like so much longer.
I miss you so much. 
I’m glad we were able to say goodbye, properly. <br>
Things around here are really strange, now. 
The void in the wake of your death is immense.
At least
For me.
People carry on as they should, 
But I don’t know that you were ever properly honored. 
Maybe that’s just because I wanted it to be so much more. 
Your death should have made a statement. 
Like your life did. 
You have to rely on those left to take care of that part. 
I let you down. 
I let them take over and do things as they saw fit. 
I regret that.
They did the best they could I guess. 
There were flowers and pictures and kind words, 
 A few shared memories, stories and tears. 
The pastor gave a sound eulogy, probably one of the better ones, and he didn’t even know you. 
I didn’t cry.
Not a single tear.
As we stood around your coffin
I watched men with shovels
Standing by a white pickup truck
Not very far down the asphalt cemetery drive
No respect. 
They were too close. 
Hovering like buzzards. 
It made me mad. 
I thought I would walk over there
calmly take one of their shovels
and smash out the windshield and headlights of the truck. 
Maybe smash one of their heads too. 
But I didn’t. 
They smoked and grinned and waited. 
They weren’t fit to throw dirt on your grave. 
But eventually they would. 
At the end of the funeral
The women all got a carnation from the big bouquet atop your casket
That was a nice touch. 
You always had an eye for the dames. 
I took one for myself until I realized they were only for the girls. 
So I had to give mine up. 
Just a flower.
As I turned and walked away I realized it was really over
And I had let you down. 
I put my empty hands in my pockets. 
They should have sprung for roses anyway. <br>
Then we ate.
The church banquet room provided meat sandwiches on buns,
Egg-potato salad, ambrosia, baked beans and little paper cups of  Kool-Aid.
Some Jell-O for dessert. 
We stared at each other with chilling smiles on folding metal chairs at round tables
And talked about how nice it was outside and how we better be getting the lawn mowed, etcetera.
What was going on with the price of gas? How about them Hawks? 
Meaningless, pointless, strained and manufactured conversations
The forced yet polite clichés of our society
Echoed right up to the rafters. 
Walking on eggshells never sounded so deafening.  <br>
I pushed around the salads on my plate
Into little mountains. 
Then flattened them. 
In my silence
I had let you down. 
The toes inside my shoes were curled up tight. 
My chest felt like the jackboots of a thousand Nazis were grinding their heels
Into my heart. 
You had fought the bastards. Nazi’s and the rest. 
You had driven troops to the front lines,
Escorted Colonels up into the mountains
You hung upside down for hours in an abandoned, bombed-out house in France
After falling through the floor.
The enemy just over the hill.  <br>
Those were great stories. 
If somebody doesn’t write them down soon
They’ll be forgotten.
The only thing people want from you now	
Is what’s left in your bank account.
The property in Florida
</div>

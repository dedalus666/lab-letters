Red Diamond Series - Rubedo

I cut all the wood for these boxes at the same time, using scrap from other projects. At the time, I was also experimenting with cutting geometric shapes out of wood as well; diamonds, triangles, odd cuts, etc and then assembling them into various collaborative shapes. Via this approach, when it came to designing the boxes, I landed upon a theme: each box would have a red diamond and the color scheme would be similar throughout all twelve boxes. 

Old alchemists described the Great Work in stages: the blackening, the clearing, the yellowing, and last the reddening, "rubedo," when a base material turns into something worth keeping. My process happens to follow that order, and so I leaned into it. The torch is the blackening. The layered teal, orange and gold stains are the clearing and yellowing, bringing the grain of the wood out, layer by layer. The red diamond, set where the eye lands first, is the final reddening. They were cut, joined, assembled, scorched, stained and finished. Scrap in, something visually striking and useful out.

Every box in the Red Diamond (Rubedo) series carries a red diamond: the final reddening. Twelve of these exist and no two match. Each has a name, a story, a burned number. Think of each as a small labyrinth, a room inside a room. Keep something in it you'd be sorry to lose, or something you'll be glad to find later. Lock away your treasures. 

I gave them all away. This is where they came from. If one of them found its way to you, I hope you enjoy it.

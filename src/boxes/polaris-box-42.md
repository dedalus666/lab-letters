Polaris - Box 42

Every labyrinth maker keeps a ledger of the people who entered it and what became of them. Most of the entries end the same way: *still searching.*

A wizened, old craftsman built this one, so he knew its flaw better than anyone. A maze is not hard because the walls are cruel. It is hard because every turn looks like the last one. After enough hours, a person forgets which way is out and starts to wonder was there was ever an out at all.

So he did what a good architect does. He didn’t fix the maze. He created a tool, that helped fix the person.

He took scraps of the same wood the walls were made from, the offcuts so often swept away and discarded, assembled a rudimentary box and adorned it with more scrap, cut into four points, resembling the star on a compass. He scored and burned the edges so the grain would remember blade and fire. Finger joints held the corners fast and bright stains gave them color. He set two red diamond shapes at the center of the star, pointing opposite directions, and two teal diamonds the same. And he gave the box to a girl named Thessaly, who was about to go in.

“It won’t tell you where the exit is,” he said. “No such thing exists, not in here. But no matter how many turns you take, one tip of this star will always face the place you came from. And one will always point the place you are going. All you have to do is keep both in mind.”

Thessaly went in. Hours passed, then days. The walls shifted; the light changed color; the corridors folded back on one another. She stopped trusting her eyes. But whenever she gazed upon the top of the box, the red diamond was there at the center, pointing to and fro, steady, like a warm, flickering flame in the window of a house far away on a hillside in the dark of night. 

On the last day, so tired she had begun to think the maze was the whole world, she sat down against a wall and opened the lid. Inside she had kept only one thing: a ribbon from her sister’s hair. She held it up in the dim light, and for the first time in days she remembered why she had come.

She stood and walked on, following the point of the diamond star. It led to a wall, but instead of turning, she stopped. Looking closely, she did not find an exit. Instead, she noticed that within the wall there was a door, which is a different thing, and it opened on a morning, and a path seldom taken to the world outside. 

*Keep something in it you’d be sorry to lose. It will remind you to keep going.*

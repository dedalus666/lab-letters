Pentacle - Box 45

Orla made boxes at the bottom of a hill, in a shop with a stove that smoked and a door that stuck in the rain. By the time the town called her old, she had made more than anyone could count, and none of them were smooth.

A boy named Teo came to sweep for her the summer he turned nine. He picked up one of her newest boxes, a square one, the corners locked together like fingers, the lid nesting down over the sides, and turned it in the light.

“There’s a mark on it,” he said. On the lid, a five-pointed star, set in teal, sat in the middle of a long, orange burn. “And here. And here.”

“Yes,” Orla said.

“You can sand those out. I’ve seen you do it with the others.”

“I could.”

“Why don’t you?”

She took the box from him and ran her thumb along the black edge of the first scar. “This one is the winter the roof fell in. I couldn’t afford to fix it, so I fixed it with scrap and made boxes out of what was left.” Her thumb moved. “This one is the year I buried my husband. I forgot the stove was lit.” Another. “This one is the day I almost quit. I put the torch to the wood just to feel something, and the grain came up red as a coal.”

Teo looked at the box for a long time.

“It’s not ruined,” he said, slowly. “It’s, like, a map.”

“Everything I’ve made is,” Orla said. “People ask what happened to me and I never have to tell them. They just have to turn the box over.”

She pointed at the star. “Do you know what this is? Old card readers call it the Pentacle. The suit of the maker, the worker, the made world. It never shows a perfect thing. It shows someone *working*, still at the bench, still hurt and still creating. A perfect thing hasn’t been through anything. It has nothing to tell you.”

The next summer Teo made his first box. It was crooked, and one of the joints gapped, and he’d scorched the lid by accident. He came to her door almost in tears.

She looked at it for a long time and smiled.

“Keep the burn, it highlights the grain. We'll fill the gaps with glue and sawdust. And then we'll cut the lid to fit,” she said. "It's not imperfect. It's one of a kind."

*Keep something in it you’d be sorry to lose. It will carry your scars as well as it carries your treasures.*

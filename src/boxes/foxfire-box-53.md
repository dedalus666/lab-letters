Foxfire - Box 53

Deep in the forest, where the old trees fall and nobody comes to move them, there is a log that glows.

It glows at night. Not much, a pale, greenish light, like a thought just before it becomes a word. The village children call it foxfire, and say it’s the breath of a fox who ran too fast through the dark and left something behind. The scientists say it’s a kind of fungus eating a kind of wood. Both are true, and the log doesn’t care which.

A red fox lived beside it. She wasn’t an ordinary fox; she collected things. A brass button. A child’s tooth. A thimble, a glass bead, a key to a lock that had long since rotted away. She tucked them into the hollow of the glowing log, one at a time, and in the dark they shone with the same faint, ghostly light, as though the log had decided to keep them too.

Nobody knew why she did it. It’s not what foxes do. But on autumn nights, when the sky went violet and the last orange light drained out of the trees, she would trot out from the underbrush with some new find in her mouth, leap lightly onto the log, and place it carefully among the others.

One evening a girl followed her. She was lost, and very tired, and she saw something pale and moving in the trees. She came into the clearing and stopped.

The fox looked at her. The girl looked at the glowing log with its small hoard of ordinary treasures, each of them lit from within.

“Those are just scraps,” the girl said.

The fox said nothing. But she nudged the thimble with her nose, and the light on it brightened, and the girl understood.

The log had once been a tree. The treasures had been things somebody threw away, lost or forgot. Every one of them was something that should have faded to nothing. And here, in the dark, with the rot and the damp and the slow work of the forest, they were glowing.

The girl found her way home that night by the foxfire. Years later, she became a woodworker, and she made twelve boxes from scrap that others had left for the fire. She stained them in layers, until the color came up out of the grain like light. She gave each one a red diamond, and gave them all away.

*Keep something in it you’d be sorry to lose. Even scrap can glow.*

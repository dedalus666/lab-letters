A series of vengeful, desperate acts
put this catastrophe into play
The anger of Gods and their jealous Kings
leads to the curse of Pasiphae

The spell fired lust in the heart of the Queen
to lay with the bull stayed from the blade
A sacrifice denied the God of the Sea
the wrath of these consequences made

An architect whose great skill would allow
a brilliant disguise in the shape of a cow
hollowed out for the Queen to repose
accepting the seed of the snow-white bull

The bastard child suffered the most
half-man and half-bull named Asterios
Condemned to a labyrinth carved of stone
wandering endless halls alone

Pasiphae:
“If I had given my body full
you could condemn me as a whore
But this was no act of my free will
this is some madness brought on by a God

What could I have seen in this animal
to assault my heart with this shameful love?
Did he look too handsome in his robe?
A sea of fire smoldering in his eyes?”

King Minos, enraged, tore out his hair
though he had lain with many a maiden fair
The wrath of the Witch-Queen’s poison would doom
each mistress the King lay, a death-bed swoon
